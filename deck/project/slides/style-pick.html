Our read on the data style, for Will to confirm. Rather than one look everywhere, each job gets the look that does it best. Oura’s arc and contributors for Today and the FloraScore. Today is the daily glance and the place FloraScore ties Florin to the score people know from Oura, which was Will’s reason for keeping FloraScore on Oct 14. The arc gauge carries the usual range on its track and a state word read against the person’s own usual. The contributors list turns the four parts into rows with a state word and a thin bar, each opening what that part is and how much it weighs, which also answers the review’s ask that the parts visibly explain the score. Field journal’s straight-axis figures for the detail pages. A detail page is where someone checks the numbers: values, ranges and counts against an axis. That is how Alex reads, and the review’s design debts (colours that change meaning, dashed lines, faint labels) are easiest to fix in a look built on plain axes and captions. Easy read as the larger-text setting. One big number and plain words, used whenever “Larger text and simpler screens” is on, whatever the data style. Rings and Bento stay in the data looks library as components to compare. Bento would also need DESIGN.md’s glass rule amended. The Oura and Easy read looks are now built in the library, and slides 18 and 19 show them. The small pictures on this slide are schematic, not data.
