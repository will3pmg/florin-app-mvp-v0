The question: how should Florin draw the numbers? This decides how FloraScore, the week and the Insights cards look; every style shows the same numbers from the same logs. The week card you flagged is being redrawn. This decision is about style, not that fix. Two of the six are the real contenders: Oura and Field journal, which we recommend using together. The other four are there to compare. The next six slides show each one live. Florin charts. What it is: Today’s dark green FloraScore card, and Insights cards that say the person’s usual in words, give a count, then draw a plain bar or dot chart. Why do it: each card says the person’s usual in words and gives a count before the chart, and every card works the same way. Why not: it’s a lot of text and small charts, so it’s slow to take in at a glance. Rings. What it is: FloraScore as a ring that fills up, with the person’s usual range marked on it, and each day of the week as its own small ring. Why do it: rings read at a glance, and a ring for each day makes a week easy to count. Why not: rings are hard to compare across cards, and a ring a day gets small over 30 days. Field journal. What it is: the numbers written out, with plain line and bar charts with labelled axes, each with a one-line caption. Why do it: it’s the most exact, so it suits detail pages and a clinician report. Why not: it’s the most reading, and its small captions are hard for older eyes. Bento. What it is: tiles of different sizes, one number each. Why do it: easy to take in at a glance, like phone widgets. Why not: tiles show numbers but not how they connect, and they crowd when text is large. Oura. What it is: a dark screen with FloraScore as a large round gauge, one line about today, and a bar for each thing the score is made from: timing, how often, consistency, comfort. Why do it: it shows the score and what it’s made from most clearly, in a layout Oura users already know. Why not: it looks like Oura’s app, so it needs Florin’s own colours and shapes, or it reads as a copy. Easy read. What it is: one big number and one plain sentence on each card, in large type. Why do it: the easiest to read, for anyone with reading glasses or little patience for charts. Why not: it leaves out the shape of the data, so it’s thin for someone who wants depth. We recommend the Oura style on Today and the FloraScore page, Field journal on detail pages, and Easy read only in the Easy version (decision 8).
