Decision 1 of 9

How does a person describe a BM’s consistency?

This decides the words on the BM tiles, and how Today, History and Insights name each BM.

A: numbers 1 to 7

What it is: Seven tiles, each a number from 1 to 7 with a short word, from the scale clinicians use.

Why do it: Clinicians and other gut apps use these numbers, so a log reads straight across at a visit.

Why not: The Rome Foundation licenses the scale, so we need a licence and must use its exact wording.

B: five words

What it is: The five words the current app uses, as tiles: Hard, Firm, Formed, Loose and Watery.

Why do it: People who use the current app already know them, and they need no licence.

Why not: Hard and Loose each cover two kinds of stool, so Florin can’t always tell when a BM has shifted from the person’s usual.

C: seven words

We recommend

What it is: Seven tiles in our own words, with no numbers: Pellets, Hard, Firm, Formed, Soft, Loose and Watery.

Why do it: All seven kinds in plain words, each with a number from 1 to 7 behind it that a clinician report can show once we have a licence.

Why not: Counsel must clear the words first, since they follow the licensed scale’s seven kinds, and people who use the current app learn two new words.

We recommend C, once counsel has read its words, and dropping B. It keeps the detail a clinician needs and the plain words a first-timer can pick, without waiting for a licence.

Still open: Do we ask the Rome Foundation now for one licence covering the 1 to 7 numbers in clinician reports and a questionnaire for the pilot study?
