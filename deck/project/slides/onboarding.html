The onboarding toggle: Short or Full survey. Both end on Today, day 1, with the answers shown in Profile. Short asks only what changes the app on day 1, in under two minutes, and every step can be skipped: a welcome in our voice with no emoji, first name, what brings you (reworded, select any), what you’d like Florin to do (up to three, reworded as what Florin does, never outcomes), periods (optional, for cycle context), reminders (a BM reminder time, and the check-in day if the check-in is on), what Florin is and isn’t (wellness, read against your own usual, alarm features go to primary care, your data is yours), then Log your first BM or Go to Today. Full survey is today’s 14 sections refreshed: age range and sex optional, weight left out, typical consistency in the chosen option’s words with “It varies” allowed, symptoms where choosing blood, black or tar-like stools or waking at night shows the Teal primary-care note on that step, eating pattern, stress and sleep, known triggers that become foods to watch, medicines and supplements as context for the clinician report, conditions self-reported with prefer not to say, and research consent as a clear, revocable choice. What today’s survey gets wrong, from the screenshots Will sent on Oct 27 (reference/shipping-app/onboarding-and-profile.md). The consistency screen names the clinical stool scale, a Rome Foundation instrument we can’t name without a licence, and labels its steps “Normal” and “Ideal”, which reads a person’s body against a norm. The conditions list names a condition our claims rules say Florin never mentions. “Bloody stool” and “Mucus in stool” are plain chips with no primary-care note. Goals promise outcomes: “Reduce IBS-D flare-ups” reads as treatment, and “Optimize microbiome health” is a claim Florin can’t measure without hardware. Weight is asked on the second screen, and weight loss is on the alarm list. Recommendation: Short, then ask the rest in context (known triggers when a food first shows up, medicines when the clinician report is made). The review notes 60% of men want setup under 10 minutes, and 14 questions is long for Marcus.
