    Easy read is the look made for a 70-year-old with reading glasses, a slight tremor and little patience for charts. Every card asks one question and answers it the same way: a short title, the number big, one plain sentence in large type, and at most one simple picture with its words written on it (a row of day circles, a few thick bars with the count on them, or one big ring). A colour always comes with its word, main text is full-strength ink, with Ink 2 only for secondary lines, and nothing a person has to read is in small caps or mono. The FloraScore card: one big ring, the only ring in the look, with 83 inside it and “Your usual 74 to 82” written under the number. Her usual is a Plum band with end ticks on the ring’s outer edge; the ring runs to 83 and ends in a Gold dot, because today is a little above her usual range. The sentence says so in words, with the same Gold dot beside it: “A little above your usual range.” Then the plain line that FloraScore compares you with you and isn’t a medical score. The stage two spec drew Easy’s FloraScore as a number with no arcs; the look keeps this one ring, with its words written inside it. The week card: “This week, Oct 5 to today”, 4 of 6 days within her usual, and “Outside your usual on Wednesday and Friday.” Seven day circles with the date inside: Plum for within her usual (Monday, Tuesday, Saturday and today), Gold for outside it (Wednesday, when her first BM came at 8:55 AM, after her usual 7:15 to 8:45 AM window, and Friday, when one BM was firmer than her usual forms), and a thin outline for Thursday, which has no logs: a gap, never a zero. The words under the circles name every state on show, so Gold never stands alone. Today’s circle is Plum while the FloraScore’s dot is Gold: the day itself sat within her usual, and the score, which blends her last 3 logged days, sits a point above her usual range. Motion: in the app each part of a card fades and rises in once, a step apart, with no sweeps and no counting. The slide plays it the same way. Strength: nothing to decode, for anyone who turns on larger text and simpler screens. Cost, our read: counts in words stand in for lines over time, so how a month moved is harder to see; that stays on the standard screens.
